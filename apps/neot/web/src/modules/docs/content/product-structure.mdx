# Product structure

Company builds independent products through one engineering platform. Each product has a clear purpose, owner, data boundary, and release cycle.

## Why products stay separate

Product separation limits risk and keeps ownership clear. A change in one product must not break another product.

Each product owns:

- its business rules and user workflows;
- its product data and files;
- its application configuration;
- its tests and release evidence;
- its deployment and maintenance decisions;
- its client support records.

## What the platform shares

The engineering platform supplies controls that every product can use:

- identity and permission rules;
- source control and code review;
- AI agent access and approval rules;
- testing and release checks;
- development and deployment environments;
- logs, health checks, and performance signals.

The platform provides common capabilities. It must not contain one product's private business rules.

## Product boundary rule

Keep a feature inside a product when only that product needs the feature. Share a capability only when several products need the same general behavior.

For example, a rule used by one approved product stays in that product. User identity can belong to the shared platform when several products need it.

## Define Project N before development

Project N stays undefined until the team records these decisions:

1. Name the industry and business problem.
2. Identify the client roles and main users.
3. Define the first complete workflow.
4. Define the product data and access rules.
5. Select the required shared capabilities.
6. Define tests, deployment, support, and maintenance ownership.

After approval, Project N becomes an independent product with its own delivery cycle.
